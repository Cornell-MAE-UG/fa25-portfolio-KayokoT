For Problem Set #4 in MAE 3240: Heat Transfer, I completed three problems that applied concepts such as thermal resistance networks and extneded surface (fin) theory to realistic engineering scenarios, including photovoltaic panels and high-temperature industrial supports. The first problem, which analyzed a photoboltaic panel, was particularly insightful in demonstrating how electrical efficiency degrades as the panel temperature increases. Promoting heat transfer away from the panel is therefore an important thermal management challenge when designing more efficient solar energy systems. Understanding the underlying governing equations and thermal resistance model for this system will be especially useful as I continue working in the Energy Research Lab to optimize and quantify the performance of a passive PV cooling system.

The second problem focused on an industrial support rod protruding from a furnace wall. This task highlighted the importance of selecting an adequate minimum length so that the temperature at the cable attachment point remained below 100°C. This provided a simple but effective example of how geometric design choices can improve safety and performance without requiring active cooling. Finally, the third problem emphasized the value of approximation in engineering analysis. By recognizing that a square pin with a convective tip condition can be approximated as a fin with an insulated tip using a corrected length, the mathematical complexity of the problem was greatly reduced while still maintaining high accuracy in the predicted heat transfer rate. This illustrated the common trade-off between model fidelity and analytical efficiency.
